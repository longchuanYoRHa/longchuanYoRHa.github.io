Year 2 In-Class Project: “Hamster Ball”

Click here to view the project website

Project Overview

The final design of the Hamster Ball

The “Hamster Ball” project was an in-class group assignment under the supervision of Dr. Heba Lakany.
It involved designing and constructing an autonomous self-balancing spherical robot using PID control to achieve stability and smooth motion.
The concept was inspired by self-balancing vehicles and aimed to apply classical control principles to a new mechanical configuration.

The robot featured a three-wheel omnidirectional platform enclosed within an acrylic sphere.
Its control system was implemented on an Arduino MEGA 2560, integrating gyroscope feedback from an MPU6050 sensor to maintain balance and perform motion control.

The Hamster Ball with controller

My Role and Contributions

I was primarily responsible for the balance control code debugging and system integration.
Starting from the traditional two-wheel self-balancing vehicle codebase, I reconstructed and adapted the control logic to suit the unique spherical configuration of the “Hamster Ball.”

Key contributions include:

  • Implemented and debugged PID-based balance control loops for roll and pitch stability.
  • Adjusted and optimized PI velocity control for omnidirectional movement.
  • Developed new drive control algorithms integrating kinematic mapping between the three omni-wheels and the spherical motion.
  • Collaborated with team members on system tuning, stability testing, and debugging to ensure smooth balance and response.

This process deepened my understanding of PID parameter tuning, control loop structure, and sensor feedback integration in embedded robotic systems.

The robot is testing its performance

Skills and Outcomes

Through this project, I gained:

  • A stronger understanding of robotics system design and closed-loop control principles.
  • Practical experience in Arduino-based real-time control and sensor fusion using the MPU6050 module.
  • Enhanced teamwork and communication skills through close collaboration with peers in mechanical, software, and electronics tasks.

The project successfully demonstrated a stable, self-balancing spherical robot, capable of omnidirectional movement within the designed control limits.

Reflection

This project marked a significant step in my growth as a robotics engineer.
It helped me translate theoretical control concepts into practice, refine my debugging methodology, and gain a deeper appreciation of the balance between mechanical design, control theory, and teamwork in achieving functional robotic systems.

“Precision in control comes not from code alone, but from understanding the system it commands.”
